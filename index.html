If it doesn't load

Three things to check, in order.

1

Same Wi-Fi network

Your phone or laptop needs to be on the same network as the ESP32, not mobile data.

2

IP hasn't shifted

Router restarts can reassign it — check your router's connected-devices list for the ESP32's current address.

3

Node is powered

The LCD should be lit and cycling readings. No display usually means a power or brownout issue at the box.

Tracked to repair, then fixed

1

Vibration is sensed

A vibration sensor on the chassis picks up the jolt as a wheel crosses a pothole — the same kind of signal a passenger would feel, just measured.

2

The IMU rules out false alarms

Speed bumps, hard turns, and gear changes shake the vehicle too. The IMU's acceleration signature tells those apart from an actual pothole hit.

3

The ESP32 does the thinking

It timestamps the event, estimates severity from peak acceleration, and decides what the LCD should show right then.

4

The dashboard updates over Wi-Fi

The node hosts its own page on the local network, so anyone connected can watch readings come in live — this page polls it when it's reachable.

5

Everything lands on the field map

Sensor detections and citizen reports become pins colored by severity. Filter them, click them, and scan the ledger below — data also exports to CSV for the department.

6

Entries get a life of their own

Each report moves through Reported → Awaiting inspection → Repair scheduled → Fixed. Push them along as work happens, and the map turns them grey when done.
